## Ergonomic Performance: The Part That Matters

![Anatomical diagram showing forearm pronation reduction at 57 degrees](/images/articles/logitech-mx-vertical-vs-anker-ergonomic-pronation-angle-diagram.jpg)
*57° reduces forearm pronation by approximately half compared to a standard flat mouse — both mice achieve this equally.*

This is the most critical section for buyers: **both mice deliver identical ergonomic benefit.**

The 57-degree angle rotates the forearm from a palm-down position to a neutral handshake position. This reduces:

- **Forearm pronation** by approximately 57%
- **Carpal tunnel pressure** — less pronation means less tunnel compression
- **Pronator muscle fatigue** — the muscles that twist your forearm are partially unloaded
- **Ulnar deviation** — wrist sits in a more neutral lateral position

The sensor quality, Bluetooth capability, and software features of the MX Vertical do **not** add additional wrist protection. The 57-degree angle does the ergonomic work, and both mice have it. For a deeper look at the relationship between vertical mice and wrist and carpal tunnel conditions, the evidence is consistent: the angle matters, not the price.

### Adjustment Period

Both mice have the same adjustment curve:
- **Days 1–3:** Cursor feels imprecise; clicking requires conscious effort
- **Days 4–7:** Basic navigation feels normal; drag-and-drop still awkward
- **Days 8–14:** Fully natural; old flat mouse feels uncomfortable when tried

Keep your flat mouse nearby for the first week for time-sensitive tasks. The transition period is identical for both mice because the grip angle — the part your muscles are adapting to — is the same.

### Grip Size and Hand Fit

One ergonomic difference that does exist between these two mice is physical size. The MX Vertical has a 78mm grip width designed for medium-to-large hands (palm width 3.0–3.5 inches). The Anker measures 64mm at the grip, better suited for medium hands (2.5–3.0 inches).

If the mouse does not fill your palm properly, you will unconsciously grip tighter, which creates its own strain. Measure your palm width before purchasing — this is more important than any feature difference.

### Research Backing the 57-Degree Angle

Published ergonomic research consistently demonstrates that vertical mice at approximately 57 degrees significantly reduce forearm pronation compared to standard horizontal mice. Studies from occupational health literature show reduced muscle activity in the pronator teres and increased neutral wrist posture. Both mice achieve this identical biomechanical outcome.

The research does not distinguish between budget and premium vertical mice. The angle is what drives the outcome. For carpal tunnel sufferers specifically, our guide to the [best vertical mouse for carpal tunnel](/guides/best-vertical-mouse-carpal-tunnel) covers the clinical evidence in detail.

## Sensor and Tracking

![Person using Logitech MX Vertical ergonomic mouse at standing desk with proper wrist posture](/images/articles/logitech-mx-vertical-vs-anker-ergonomic-mx-vertical-desk-use.jpg)
*Both mice deliver identical wrist posture — the sensor quality determines tracking surfaces, not ergonomic benefit.*

### Logitech MX Vertical: Darkfield Sensor

The Darkfield sensor tracks on virtually any surface:

| Surface | MX Vertical | Anker |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric mouse pad | Excellent | Excellent |
| Wood desk | Excellent | Good |
| **Glass desk** | **Excellent** | **Fails** |
| Laminate | Excellent | Good |
| Marble/stone | Excellent | Fails |

4000 DPI maximum with precise adjustment in Logi Options+. Running at 1600 DPI means using 40% of sensor capacity — smooth and precise. The higher DPI ceiling also means the MX Vertical can handle 4K and ultrawide monitors where larger cursor travel distances require faster tracking.

### Anker: Standard Optical Sensor

The Anker's optical sensor is perfectly adequate for standard surfaces. 1600 DPI maximum covers all office tasks. Three preset DPI levels (800/1200/1600) cover most scenarios, and a small LED on the top of the mouse indicates the current setting.

**The glass tracking limitation is the only real-world scenario where the sensor creates a problem.** If your desk is standard wood or laminate with a mouse pad, the Anker tracks as well as the MX Vertical in everyday use. The difference only shows at the extremes — glass surfaces, very fast movements, and high-resolution multi-monitor setups.

### DPI and Cursor Speed in Practice

For standard office work on a single 1080p or 1440p monitor, 1200–1600 DPI is the sweet spot. Both mice cover this range. Where the MX Vertical pulls ahead is fine-grained DPI adjustment — Logi Options+ lets you set any value from 400 to 4000, while the Anker locks you into three presets. If you work across a 4K monitor or dual-screen setup, the MX Vertical's higher DPI ceiling provides smoother cursor movement across large screen real estate.

## Connectivity and Multi-Device

![Anker ergonomic mouse bottom view showing USB nano dongle storage](/images/articles/logitech-mx-vertical-vs-anker-ergonomic-anker-bottom-dongle.jpg)
*The Anker stores its nano USB dongle inside the battery compartment — easy to keep safe, but limited to one computer.*

### MX Vertical: 3-Device Flexibility

- **Bluetooth** (3 pairings): no dongle, no port used
- **Unifying Receiver** (1 device via USB-A dongle)
- Switch between devices with a button on the bottom — 1–2 seconds

This matters for:
- **Desk + laptop workflow**: button press switches instantly
- **Mac + iPad**: same mouse works on both without adapter
- **Home + office**: pair once to each location's computer

With Logitech Flow enabled, the cursor crosses from one computer screen to another automatically. Files drag cross-platform. This turns the MX Vertical from a mouse into a multi-device workflow tool.

### Anker: One Computer, One Dongle

The 2.4 GHz dongle connects to one computer. Switching requires unplugging and re-plugging the dongle. The connection itself is reliable and lag-free — the limitation is strictly the one-device cap.

**For MacBook Pro users**: the Anker's USB-A dongle needs an adapter. The MX Vertical connects via Bluetooth natively — zero adapters, zero ports used. This alone justifies the price difference for many Mac users.

### Connection Reliability

Both mice use 2.4 GHz wireless, which provides virtually identical latency (around 1ms). The MX Vertical's Bluetooth option introduces slightly higher latency (3–5ms) but remains imperceptible for office tasks. Bluetooth range is approximately 10 meters; the Anker's dongle range is similar. Neither mouse drops connection during normal use at desk distance.

*Logi Options+ provides per-application button customization — different shortcuts in Excel vs Chrome vs Figma.*

### Logi Options+ (MX Vertical Only)

Key features:
- **Per-app button customization**: different actions per application
- **Gesture button**: hold and move in 4 directions = 4 additional macOS/Windows actions
- **DPI slider**: set any value from 400–4000
- **Logitech Flow**: cursor crosses between computers seamlessly
- **SmartActions**: automated task sequences triggered by a single button press

The per-app customization is where the MX Vertical delivers genuine productivity value. For example, the forward/back buttons can control browser navigation in Chrome, undo/redo in Photoshop, and slide navigation in PowerPoint — all without manual switching.

### Anker: Zero Software

Plug-and-play. No software, no app, no account required. DPI button cycles through three presets with an LED indicator. This is either a feature (simplicity, privacy, no telemetry) or a limitation (no customization, no per-app profiles) depending on your needs.

For users who prefer not to install manufacturer software — especially in corporate environments with software restrictions — the Anker's driver-free operation is a meaningful advantage.

## Mac Users: Which to Choose

MacBook Pro has no USB-A ports. The Anker's USB-A dongle requires an adapter, takes a USB-C port, and is easy to lose. The MX Vertical connects via Bluetooth — no adapter, no ports used, charges via the same USB-C cable as your MacBook.

Logi Options+ runs natively on macOS (Apple Silicon optimized) with per-app button customization mapping to Mission Control, Expose, Spaces, and Launchpad. The gesture button adds four directional shortcuts — hold and swipe up for Mission Control, down for App Expose, left for previous Desktop, right for next Desktop.

### MacBook Pro Compatibility Breakdown

- **MX Vertical + MacBook Pro**: Bluetooth pairs in 10 seconds. USB-C charges with existing Mac cable. Logi Options+ installs from web. No adapters, no dongles, no ports consumed.
- **Anker + MacBook Pro**: Requires USB-A to USB-C adapter (~$8). Occupies one USB-C port. No native macOS software. Functional but clunky integration.

**Verdict for Mac users**: MX Vertical is significantly better. The seamless Bluetooth pairing and native software make it worth the premium for any Mac-centric workflow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Logitech MX Vertical worth the extra cost over the Anker?**

Yes, if you need Bluetooth multi-device pairing, Darkfield glass tracking, Logi Options+ customization, or USB-C charging. No, if you only need ergonomic wrist relief — the Anker provides the same 57-degree pronation reduction for $65 less.

**Do both mice have the same ergonomic angle?**

Yes. Both use a 57-degree handshake grip angle, providing approximately 57% pronation reduction compared to a flat mouse. The ergonomic benefit is identical.

**Can the Anker mouse track on a glass desk?**

No. The standard optical sensor cannot track on glass. You need a mouse pad or opaque desk surface. The MX Vertical's Darkfield sensor tracks on glass without a mouse pad.

**Which mouse has better battery life?**

The Anker lasts approximately 6 months on two AAA batteries. The MX Vertical lasts approximately 4 months on its built-in rechargeable battery, with USB-C charging and 1-minute quick charge providing 3 hours of use.

**Which is better for Mac users?**

The Logitech MX Vertical — Bluetooth (no dongle), USB-C charging matching Mac cables, and native Logi Options+ on macOS with gesture support.

**How long does it take to adjust to a vertical mouse?**

Most users adjust within 7 to 14 days. Days 1–3 feel imprecise, days 4–7 feel mostly normal, and by day 14 the vertical position feels completely natural. Keep your flat mouse nearby the first week for time-sensitive tasks.

**Can I use either mouse on multiple computers?**

The MX Vertical supports 3 Bluetooth pairings — switch between computers with a button press. The Anker connects to one computer at a time via its 2.4 GHz dongle.

**Which mouse is better for large hands?**

The MX Vertical at 78mm grip width suits medium-to-large hands (palm width 3.0–3.5 inches). The Anker at 64mm suits medium hands (2.5–3.0 inches). If your hands are larger than 3.5 inches, consider the Evoluent VerticalMouse 4 which offers the widest grip profile.

**Which mouse should I buy first?**

If you have never used a vertical mouse before, start with the Anker at $25. Same ergonomic angle, minimal cost to test the concept. Upgrade to MX Vertical if you need the premium features.

**What is the main practical difference between the two mice?**

The Anker provides the same ergonomic angle for $65 less. The MX Vertical adds Bluetooth, glass tracking, 3-device multi-pairing, Logi Options+ software, quieter clicks, and USB-C charging. Choose Anker for value; choose MX Vertical for full-featured convenience.
